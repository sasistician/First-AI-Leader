The Math Underneath · Part 1 of 4 04 — 11
the building block you already know

Every neural network starts as something you've seen before.

Step 1 y = wx + b Linear regression A weighted sum of inputs, plus a bias. The simplest possible model — fit a line through data.
→
Step 2 + activation Nonlinearity Squash the output (ReLU, sigmoid...) so the model can learn curves and interactions, not just straight lines.
→
Step 3 stack layers Deep network Chain dozens of these together. Each layer learns a progressively more abstract pattern.
→
Step 4 + attention Transformer Add one new layer type — attention, plus a way to encode word order. That's the key ingredient behind today's LLMs.

Nothing magic — just linear algebra, stacked deep enough, with one new layer type. Next: that one new layer type.

The Paper That Changed Everything · Part 2 of 4 05 — 11
2017 · "attention is all you need" · google research

Before 2017, models read one word at a time — in order, like a tape.

  • Pre-2017 — RNNs & LSTMs: process tokens sequentially, one after another. Slow to train, and they "forget" context from many words back.
  • 2017 — the Transformer: replaces recurrence entirely with self-attention — every token is processed in parallel, and can directly relate to every other token, no matter how far apart.
  • The result: training finally scales — more data, more compute, more parameters — leading directly to GPT, Claude, and everything since.

This is the single architectural change that made the last decade of AI possible.

How Attention Works · Part 2 of 4 06 — 11
the intuition, no math required

Every word looks at every other word — and decides what matters.

The agent fixed the bug in the file

"Bug" attends strongly to "agent," "fixed," and "file" — the words that give it meaning in this sentence — and barely to "the." That's attention: each word decides which other words matter to it.

  • Each word asks a question — "what do I need to understand myself?" — that's the Query.
  • Each word also advertises what it offers — "here's what I'm about" — that's the Key.
  • Words whose advertisement matches the question get more attention, weighted by relevance — the Value that flows through.
  • Repeat this across many layers, and the model isn't just predicting the next word — it's building a working understanding of the whole context. That's what lets it decide what to do next.
The Bridge · Part 3 of 4 07 — 11
from autocomplete to agency

At its core, Claude is still just predicting the next word. So how does it edit files, run tests, and fix bugs?

  • Next-word prediction, at small scale → autocomplete.
  • Next-word prediction, at huge scale, trained on the right data (code, reasoning, conversation) → planning, reasoning, working code.
  • But a model alone can't do anything — it can only produce text.
  • Something has to take that text and turn it into real actions: reading files, running commands, calling tools, and feeding the results back in.

That "something" is the agent harness — what we cover next.

The Harness · Part 4 of 4 08 — 11
the missing piece between a model and an agent

A model predicts text. A harness turns predictions into action — and feeds the results back in.

01 Perceive Read the current state — your prompt, project files, terminal output, results so far.
02 Plan The model decides what to do next — which tool, what code, what command.
03 Act The harness executes it for real — edits a file, runs a command, calls an API.
04 Observe The result feeds back as new context — and the loop repeats.
↻ perceive → plan → act → observe → repeats until the task is done
Anatomy · Part 4 of 4 09 — 11
five components, one pattern

Every agent harness — Claude Code or otherwise — is built from the same five pieces.

01Model
What it is

The LLM itself.

Role in the loop

Generates the "plan" step — what to do next, in natural language or structured tool calls.

02Tools
What it is

Functions the harness can execute.

Role in the loop

Read/write files, run shell commands, call APIs, search the web — the "Act" step.

03Context
What it is

What the model can currently "see".

Role in the loop

Your prompt, project files, CLAUDE.md, conversation history — the "Perceive" step.

04Permissions
What it is

Rules for what the harness may do unsupervised.

Role in the loop

Read-only vs. write, auto-approve vs. ask-first — the guardrails on "Act".

05Memory
What it is

What persists between sessions.

Role in the loop

Saved facts, preferences, project state — context that outlives any one loop.
